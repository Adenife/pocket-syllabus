# Fundamentals of Physics and Biology

This mock introductory curriculum covers energy, motion, cells, and ecosystems. It is original sample material for testing PocketSyllabus.

## Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's first law states that an object remains at rest or continues moving at constant velocity unless a net external force acts on it. Inertia is an object's resistance to changes in motion.

Newton's second law relates net force, mass, and acceleration: F = ma. A larger force produces greater acceleration, while a larger mass requires more force for the same acceleration. Newton's third law states that interacting objects exert equal and opposite forces on each other.

## Energy and Conservation

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion and is calculated as one half of mass times speed squared. Gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface is mass times gravitational acceleration times height.

Energy can change form but is conserved in an isolated system. Friction converts some mechanical energy into thermal energy. Power measures how quickly energy is transferred and is expressed in watts.

## Waves and Sound

Waves transfer energy without transporting matter over long distances. Wavelength is the distance between successive crests; frequency counts oscillations per second. Wave speed equals frequency multiplied by wavelength.

Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a medium such as air, water, or a solid. Higher frequency generally corresponds to higher pitch, while greater amplitude corresponds to louder sound.

## Cells and Their Organelles

Cells are the basic units of life. A cell membrane regulates substances entering and leaving the cell. The nucleus of a eukaryotic cell contains DNA, which carries hereditary information.

Mitochondria support cellular respiration, releasing usable energy from nutrients. Plant cells also contain chloroplasts for photosynthesis and a cellulose cell wall that provides structural support.

## Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration

Photosynthesis uses light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into sugars, releasing oxygen. Chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light. Sugars store chemical energy that supports plant growth and feeds other organisms.

Cellular respiration transfers energy from glucose to ATP, which powers many cellular processes. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and produces carbon dioxide and water. Plants perform both photosynthesis and cellular respiration.

## Ecosystems and Food Webs

An ecosystem includes organisms and their physical environment. Producers, such as plants, capture energy; consumers obtain energy by eating other organisms; decomposers recycle nutrients from dead material.

Food webs connect multiple feeding relationships. Energy available to organisms decreases across trophic levels because organisms use energy for metabolism and release heat. Nutrients cycle through organisms, soil, water, and the atmosphere.
